# Results: loopback capture with forced 2 ms render period

2026-10-28, Windows, .NET 10 x64 Debug with one-second warmup. No rendering/GPU workload.
Default endpoint: **Speakers (USB Audio CODEC)**, 48,000 Hz stereo 32-bit float.
Supported shared period: minimum 96 frames / 2 ms, default 480 frames / 10 ms.

**None of the three alternate implementations made the render-period trick reduce the
observed loopback delivery interval to 2 ms on this endpoint.**

## Main comparisons

Five-second measurements, ordinary silent playback present in every baseline.
All forced cases verified a current engine period of 96 frames / 2 ms at the start and
end of measurement. All ordinary baselines verified 480 frames / 10 ms.

| Capture implementation | Baseline delivery | Forced engine delivery | What the callbacks contained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NAudio 3.4.0-preview.1 (control) | ~100 callbacks/s | ~100 callbacks/s | 480 frames / 10 ms |
| CSCore 1.2.1.2 | ~100 callbacks/s | ~100 callbacks/s | 480 frames / 10 ms |
| BASSWASAPI 2.4.4.1, BASS 2.4.18.3 | ~100 callbacks/s | ~100 callbacks/s | 480 frames / 10 ms |
| Hexa.NET.MiniAudio 1.0.1 | ~136 callbacks/s, ~100 delivery bursts/s | Same | 352-frame / 7.333 ms callbacks, with extra callbacks arriving in bursts |

The miniaudio result is an example of why callback size alone is insufficient. A forced
five-second capture produced 682 callbacks but only 500 delivery bursts. The 182 additional
callbacks arrived within 0.25 ms of another callback. Median arrival gap was 9.982 ms.
Baseline behavior was the same. Its internal capture period was 352 frames / three periods,
even with fixed-sized callback adaptation disabled.

Fresh-process capture-first repeats explicitly verified **10 ms before capture/helper,
then 2 ms after starting the helper**:

| Implementation | Callbacks in 5 s | Delivery bursts | Median callback size | Median arrival gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CSCore | 500 | 500 | 10 ms | 10.4319 ms |
| BASSWASAPI | 500 | 500 | 10 ms | 10.0015 ms |
| miniaudio | 682 | 500 | 7.333 ms | 9.9783 ms |

This rules out startup order as the explanation for the negative result. Some initial
exploratory cases started while Windows was still retaining the previous process's 2 ms
period; they are retained in `matrix`, but the `clean` repeats correct that ambiguity.

## Additional configurations

Four-second runs, with actual engine period verified at 2 ms:

- CSCore event-driven loopback: 400 callbacks, all 10 ms; median gap 10.0002 ms.
- CSCore zero-latency polling request (as in the old adapter): 398 callbacks, median block
  10 ms and median gap 10.4083 ms.
- BASSWASAPI event-driven loopback initialization: rejected, BASS error 37.
- BASSWASAPI direct 2 ms request (`buffer=0`, `period=.002`): rejected, BASS error -1.
- miniaudio with automatic format conversion disabled: 545 callbacks but 400 delivery
  bursts; still approximately 10 ms between fresh bursts.
- miniaudio requesting 96 frames / two periods: internal period became 528 frames. Callbacks
  split the data into 16/512-frame portions, paired into 528-frame / 11 ms bursts. 730
  callbacks represented 365 bursts in four seconds, with gaps extending to ~20 ms.
  This configuration did not provide 2 ms availability either.

Without 1 ms timer resolution, preliminary CSCore/BASS polling runs showed ~15.6 ms
scheduling effects and batching. The controlled comparisons above all enable 1 ms timer
resolution; event-based CSCore was also tested to avoid attributing a polling delay to WASAPI.

## Scope and artifacts

This is a result for the tested endpoint, driver, library versions, and configurations.
It is not proof of a universal 10 ms floor for Windows loopback, nor an end-to-end audio
latency measurement. No low-latency silent helper was added to production AEther.

Raw files under `artifacts/capture-backends/`:

- `matrix/`: baseline and helper-first comparisons, plus exploratory capture-first cases.
- `clean/`: capture-first runs starting from a confirmed 10 ms period.
- `variants/`: additional configurations.
- `*-*.log`: engine snapshots, detailed settings, or initialization failures.

Versions are pinned in the probe project. Native archives came from official Un4seen URLs:

- `https://www.un4seen.com/files/bass24.zip`
  SHA256 `3A03EC9A33D0F4F9D167660DA51C8BB1432E8977496995455AB137277D69636E`

  SHA256 `74B754A925FEDED1DDD6B8B7B1A37BDC9B185E86ABEBDD46B1A7E0FF67815779`

All helper streams are disposed at the end of each case. Engine settings can persist
briefly while other clients still use the endpoint; this is why baseline checks wait.
